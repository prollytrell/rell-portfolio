Patch Reports Are Only as Good as the Asset List Underneath

EDR, patch management, and vulnerability scanning tools each maintain their own view of the environment. Reconciling them is the first step in vulnerability management.

By Terrell Grenyion • 2026 • ~2 min read
← Back to Blog

I have recently taken on a larger role in patching and vulnerability management at work. One of the earliest lessons has also been one of the most important: before deciding which patches to deploy, or when, you need an accurate picture of every device you are responsible for.

One environment, several versions of the truth

That sounds simple, but most environments rely on several tools, and each maintains its own view of what exists. An EDR console, a patch management platform, and a vulnerability scanner can each present a slightly different inventory depending on how often devices check in, when they were last online, and how each tool counts them. Understanding why those views differ turned out to be a larger part of the work than I expected.

Habits that keep inventories aligned

A few practices have helped me keep these sources consistent:

  • Designate one source of truth for whether a device is active.
  • Compare the other tools against that source on a regular schedule.
  • Review devices that have not checked in for an extended period, then remediate or retire them.
  • Investigate any device that appears in one tool but not another.

Conclusion

This is not the most glamorous part of the discipline, but it reinforced something I had heard often in theory and now understand in practice: asset management is the foundation. Every patch report is only as accurate as the asset inventory beneath it.

For anyone looking to enter vulnerability management, I would recommend starting here. Learn to reconcile inventories across tools and to ask why they disagree. Everything else depends on it.
