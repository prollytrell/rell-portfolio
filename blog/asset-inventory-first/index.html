Patch Reports Are Only as Good as the Asset List Underneath

EDR, patch, and vulnerability tools each keep their own view of what exists. Reconciling them is the first job in vulnerability management.

By Terrell Grenyion • 2026 • ~2 min read
← Back to Blog

So, I've become more involved in patching and vulnerability management at work. It's been a great learning experience so far, and I want to share one of the first lessons that stood out. Before you can decide what patches to deploy or when, you need a clear picture of every device you're responsible for.

That sounds simple, but most environments rely on several tools, and each one keeps its own view of what exists. An EDR console, a patch tool, and a vulnerability scanner can each show a slightly different picture depending on how often devices check in, when they were last online, and how each tool counts them. Understanding why those views differ was a bigger part of the work than I expected. A few habits I've picked up that help keep everything lined up:

  • Pick one source of truth for whether a device is active.
  • Compare other tools against it regularly.
  • Review devices that haven't checked in for an extended period, then fix or retire them.
  • Follow up on anything that shows up in one tool but not another.

This isn't necessarily the most interesting part, but it reinforced something I've heard a lot in theory and now understand better. Asset management really is the foundation, as every patch report is only as accurate as the asset list underneath it.

If you're looking to get into vulnerability management, I'd start here. Learn how to reconcile inventories across tools and ask why they are inconsistent. This is the foundation everything else depends on.
