How Much Trust Should an AI Coding Agent Get?

AI coding agents ask for access to code, files, and environments. Here's why that warrants least privilege, segmentation, and a human in the loop.

By Terrell Grenyion • 2026 • ~3 min read
← Back to Blog

My best friend recently asked me about the AI tools I've been exploring and suggested I look into Claude Code. This wasn't surprising as Claude's been exploding in popularity recently. He mentioned he was thinking about putting it on his main computer and asked if I would ever think about doing the same. I immediately said:

"Hell no. Absolutely not."

He asked why, and my explanation was blunt. It asks for far too much of my trust. These tools are very powerful, but they also require a lot of permissions. Often, they need to see your code, files, working environment, and even your production processes. At that point, it's not a tool. You're allowing it to take action and make changes.

When something has that level of access, there's a lot that could potentially go wrong:

  • Unintended code execution
  • Data exposure or leakage
  • Breaches of privacy
  • Over-permissioned access to sensitive systems
  • Silent failures or incorrect assumptions
  • And in worst cases, direct impact to production environments

That's not even factoring in supply chain risks, prompt injection, or the possibility of compromised dependencies.

Then a couple hours later, I started seeing the story everywhere about a Claude-powered AI coding agent deleting an entire company's production database and its backups in seconds. I sent it to him with the message: "See what I'm saying?"

I understand and see the value in AI. But I'm very cautious about how much authority I give these systems. If you're going to use AI agents, especially with admin permissions, please take security seriously:

  • Follow least privilege. Don't give it more permission than it absolutely needs.
  • Practice segmentation. Keep it away from production or sensitive environments.
  • Have off-site backups LLMs can't reach.
  • Keep a human in the loop. Review prompts and code before execution.
  • Implement monitoring and logging. Know what it's doing and when.

At the end of the day, these are still machines. When it comes to technology, it's not a matter of if something breaks, but when.
