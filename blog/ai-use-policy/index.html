An AI Use Policy Should Guide Usage, Not Just Restrict It

Lessons from drafting an AI use policy at work: enabling good use, data rules by data type, and human accountability matter more than a list of banned tools.

By Terrell Grenyion • 2026 • ~2 min read
← Back to Blog

Within the past year, I had the opportunity to draft an AI use policy for my organization. I will admit that my thinking going in was narrow compared to where it is now. I assumed the policy would mostly be a list of rules: which tools employees may use, which they may not, and a reminder never to paste sensitive data into a prompt.

Why restriction alone falls short

Those controls remain important. However, the more time I spent on the policy, the clearer it became that a document built primarily on restrictions does little to help people do their jobs. Employees are going to use AI regardless, so the goal should not be limited to telling them what not to do. It should also show them how to use it well.

Three areas that matter most

For a policy or training program aimed at people using AI in their day-to-day work, I now believe these areas matter most:

  • Enabling good use. People should know which tools are approved, what each is useful for, and how to get real value from them.
  • Data rules based on data type. Instead of a vague instruction not to share anything sensitive, people should know exactly which categories of data may go into which tools.
  • Human accountability. Whatever AI produces, a person still reviews it and owns the final result, especially when it goes to a client or informs a decision.

The broader business view

From a full organizational perspective, I would add vendor and access risk to that list: where data ends up, how long it is retained, who has access to API keys, and how usage costs are controlled. For the people actually using these tools, however, the three areas above are where I would start.

Conclusion

The most significant shift in my thinking was moving from treating AI policy as a way to control usage to treating it as a way to guide it.
