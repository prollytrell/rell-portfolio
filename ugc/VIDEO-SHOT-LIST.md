# Six portfolio samples to record first

The fastest credible launch is two tech-forward samples plus four formats that prove you can work beyond tech. Aim for 20–35 seconds each. These are spec samples: do not imply the featured brand hired you.

## Repeatable structure

Use this as a starting rhythm, not a rigid script:

- `0–2 sec`: visual or spoken hook
- `2–7 sec`: problem, friction, or desired outcome
- `7–22 sec`: product in use; show, do not only tell
- `22–30 sec`: payoff and specific call to action

Record at least three hook takes and two CTA takes for every concept. That material is also useful when pitching hook variations as a paid add-on.

## 01 — Tech product demo

**Filename:** `01-tech-demo.mp4`

Choose a physical product you already use: camera accessory, microphone, keyboard, smart-home device, charger, or another gadget.

- Open on the product solving a visible problem.
- Name one frustrating old behavior.
- Demonstrate two useful features in close-up.
- Translate each feature into a practical benefit.
- Close with who should consider it.

**Editing:** fast cold open, clean captions, two or three punch-ins, macro B-roll, light sound design.

## 02 — App or SaaS walkthrough

**Filename:** `02-app-walkthrough.mp4`

Use an app or web tool you genuinely know. Avoid exposing work, client, school, or personal data in the screen recording.

- Start on camera with a result-oriented hook.
- Cut to a clean screen recording.
- Walk through one workflow, not the entire product.
- Use callouts to direct the eye.
- Return on camera for the takeaway and CTA.

**Editing:** picture-in-picture or alternating talking head and screen capture, cursor emphasis, concise text callouts.

## 03 — Problem → solution

**Filename:** `03-problem-solution.mp4`

Choose an ordinary consumer product with an obvious before-and-after: organization, cleaning, kitchen, travel, grooming, or desk setup.

- Show the frustration before naming the product.
- Introduce the product as the change.
- Demonstrate it in the real environment.
- End on the improved state.

**Editing:** visual hook before dialogue, match cut or before/after, captions that emphasize the benefit rather than repeating every word.

## 04 — Direct testimonial

**Filename:** `04-testimonial.mp4`

Choose something you have used long enough to discuss honestly. Do not invent results.

- Use a natural “I was skeptical because…” or “What surprised me…” opening.
- Give one concrete reason you kept using it.
- Include a product close-up or cutaway while you speak.
- State who it is and is not for.

**Editing:** conversational pacing, subtle jump cuts, one pattern interrupt, low-key captions. The performance should feel more important than the effects.

## 05 — Routine integration

**Filename:** `05-routine-integration.mp4`

Choose a product that belongs naturally in a morning, work, fitness, grooming, cooking, or evening routine.

- Open with the finished routine moment.
- Show where the product enters the routine.
- Include hands, environment, and on-camera use.
- Explain why the product earns its place.

**Editing:** four to six short B-roll shots, voiceover, ambient sound, clean pace, a simple end card.

## 06 — Visual showcase + voiceover

**Filename:** `06-visual-showcase.mp4`

Choose the most visually interesting product available to you.

- Capture a wide establishing shot.
- Capture medium use shots.
- Capture close detail shots, packaging, texture, controls, or movement.
- Write the voiceover after reviewing the footage so every line matches something visible.

**Editing:** movement-matched cuts, natural sound, restrained speed ramps if useful, no effect added only for decoration.

## Camera and framing checklist

- Record vertical when possible; leave safe space for captions and platform UI.
- Lock exposure and white balance so shots match.
- Prioritize clear audio over camera sophistication.
- Use window light or one soft key light; avoid mixed color temperatures.
- Capture 4K masters, then export 1080 × 1920 web copies.
- Hold every B-roll shot for 5–8 seconds even if the final cut uses less than one second.
- Record room tone and a few clean product sounds.
- Check the background for addresses, badges, work screens, mail, and other private information.

## Launch order if time is tight

Record `01`, `02`, and `03` first. Those three show your strongest differentiator, screen-demo ability, and range. Publish the page only after every visible slot is filled; until then, use a private local preview rather than sending the placeholder version to buyers.
